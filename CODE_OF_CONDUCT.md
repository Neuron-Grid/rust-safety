# Code of Conduct

Project discussions should stay technical, specific, and respectful.

## Expected conduct

Contributors are expected to:

- review ideas and code rather than attacking individuals;
- provide reproducible technical reasoning for safety and correctness claims;
- distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions or preferences;
- avoid harassment, discriminatory conduct, threats, stalking, or deliberate disruption;
- avoid publishing another person's private information or security-sensitive material without authorization;
- respect a maintainer's request to move security-sensitive material to the private process in `SECURITY.md`.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to project-controlled spaces, including issues, pull/merge requests, reviews, discussions, and other project communication channels. It also applies when a person is formally representing the project in another public space.

## Reporting conduct issues

Do not publish sensitive conduct reports, private information, or evidence of harassment in a public issue.

- **GitLab:** use a **Confidential Issue** and clearly identify the report as a Code of Conduct matter.
- **GitHub:** use a private maintainer contact method published by the repository or maintainer when one is available. GitHub's platform abuse-reporting mechanisms may also be appropriate for threats, harassment, doxxing, or platform-policy violations.
- **No private project channel available:** open only a minimal public request asking for a private maintainer contact channel. Do not include names of affected private individuals, screenshots, private messages, or other sensitive evidence in that public request.

A report should include only the information reasonably necessary to understand the conduct, where it occurred, relevant dates, and supporting evidence. Reporters should avoid collecting or distributing additional private data solely for a report.

Security vulnerabilities and undisclosed exploit material follow `SECURITY.md`, not this conduct-reporting process.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take proportionate action including a private warning, content editing/removal, thread locking, temporary participation restrictions, or a permanent ban from project-controlled spaces.

Enforcement decisions should:

1. be based on the observed conduct and available evidence rather than disagreement over technical conclusions;
2. minimize redistribution of private or sensitive report material;
3. document the reason and action internally when practical;
4. avoid public disclosure of reporter or affected-person identities unless they have consented or disclosure is required for safety or law;
5. be reviewed by a maintainer who is not the subject of the report whenever another maintainer is available.

A maintainer who is the subject of a report must not make the enforcement decision on that report when an uninvolved maintainer can handle it. If the project has only one maintainer, that maintainer should seek a neutral external reviewer where practical before taking a disputed enforcement action.

A person subject to an enforcement action may request reconsideration through the same private channel. An uninvolved maintainer should handle the reconsideration when one is available.
